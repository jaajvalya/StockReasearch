## 1. What an Equity Research Analyst Actually Does

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Follow a set of 10–25 companies, usually within one sector |
| **Modeling** | Build and maintain 3-statement financial models and forecasts |
| **Valuation** | Estimate fair value using DCF, multiples, SOTP, etc. |
| **Earnings work** | Preview, then rapidly analyse quarterly results and update estimates |
| **Primary research** | Talk to management, suppliers, customers, channel checks, industry experts |
| **Writing** | Initiation reports, earnings notes, thematic/sector reports |
| **Recommendation** | Issue Buy / Hold / Sell ratings with target prices and a clear thesis |
| **Communication** | Pitch ideas to portfolio managers, sales teams, and clients |

### Sell-side vs Buy-side

| | Sell-side | Buy-side |
|---|---|---|
| **Employer** | Brokerages, investment banks | Mutual funds, hedge funds, PMS, insurers, family offices |
| **Output** | Published reports for clients | Internal recommendations to PMs |
| **Focus** | Depth on a sector, visibility, client service | Generating returns, breadth, conviction |
| **Typical entry** | Research Associate | Research Analyst / Investment Analyst |

> Many people start on the sell-side (structured training, broad exposure) and move to the buy-side later.

### Core competencies

1. **Accounting** — read and dissect financial statements
2. **Finance & valuation** — turn analysis into a price
3. **Business/strategy judgment** — understand competitive advantage and industry structure
4. **Excel / modeling** — fast, accurate, auditable models
5. **Writing & speaking** — concise, persuasive, evidence-based
6. **Skepticism & curiosity** — question management narratives and consensus

## 2. Learning Philosophy & Approach

### Principles

1. **Learn by doing, not by reading alone.** Every concept should end in a spreadsheet, a calculation, or a written note.
2. **Use real companies from day one.** Pick 2–3 listed companies you understand as a consumer and use them as your "lab" throughout every phase.
3. **Primary sources first.** Annual reports, 10-K/10-Q (or local equivalents), earnings call transcripts, and investor presentations beat blogs and YouTube summaries.
4. **Spaced repetition for fundamentals.** Accounting formulas, ratio definitions, and valuation mechanics should be second nature — use flashcards (Anki).
5. **Write to think.** If you can't explain a thesis in one paragraph, you don't understand it yet.
6. **Get feedback.** Share your work with mentors, online communities, or study partners. Unreviewed work hides mistakes.
7. **Track your calls.** Keep a log of every recommendation with date, price, thesis, and outcome. Review it quarterly — this builds judgment.

### The learning loop (apply to every topic)

```
  Learn concept  →  Apply to real company  →  Write a short note  →  Get feedback  →  Revise
       ↑                                                                               │
       └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Time commitment

| Profile | Weekly hours | Timeline to job-ready |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time student / career break | 30–40 hrs | 6–9 months |
| Working professional (finance-adjacent) | 12–15 hrs | 12 months |
| Working professional (non-finance) | 10–15 hrs | 12–18 months |

## Phase 1 — Foundations (Months 0–3)

**Goal:** Understand how markets work, speak the language of finance, and become comfortable in Excel.

### 1.1 Financial markets basics
- Equity, debt, derivatives, money markets — what each is and who uses them
- How stock exchanges work: primary vs secondary markets, IPOs, order types, market makers
- Market indices and how they are constructed
- Market participants: retail, institutional, FIIs/FPIs, DIIs, hedge funds, regulators
- Corporate actions: dividends, splits, bonuses, buybacks, rights issues, mergers
- Market regulators and their role (e.g., SEC in the US, SEBI in India, FCA in the UK)

### 1.2 Accounting fundamentals
- Double-entry bookkeeping, accrual vs cash accounting
- The three statements and how they link
- Revenue recognition, matching principle, depreciation & amortisation
- Inventory methods (FIFO, weighted average)
- Basics of IFRS vs US GAAP (and local GAAP / Ind AS where relevant)

### 1.3 Corporate finance & economics
- Time value of money, NPV, IRR, compounding
- Risk and return, diversification, CAPM, beta
- Cost of capital (cost of equity, cost of debt, WACC) — conceptual level
- Macro: GDP, inflation, interest rates, central bank policy, currency, business cycles
- Micro: supply/demand, pricing power, market structures (monopoly, oligopoly, competition)

### 1.4 Excel fundamentals
- Navigation shortcuts (aim for mouse-free work)
- Core functions: `SUM`, `IF`, `INDEX/MATCH`, `XLOOKUP`, `SUMIFS`, `IFERROR`, `NPV`, `XNPV`, `IRR`, `XIRR`
- Absolute vs relative references, named ranges
- Data tables, goal seek, basic charts
- Formatting conventions: blue = inputs, black = formulas, green = links to other sheets

### Phase 1 resources
| Type | Resource |
|---|---|
| Course | Khan Academy — Finance & Capital Markets (free) |
| Course | Coursera / edX — Introduction to Financial Accounting (Wharton) |
| Book | *The Intelligent Investor* — Benjamin Graham |
| Book | *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* — Burton Malkiel (for market context) |
| Excel | Chandoo.org, ExcelJet, or a structured Excel-for-finance course |
| Daily | Financial Times / WSJ / Bloomberg / Economic Times / Mint (choose your market) |

### Phase 1 deliverables
- [ ] Glossary of 150+ financial terms in your own words (Anki deck)
- [ ] Hand-built linked 3-statement example for a simple fictional business
- [ ] One-page summary of 3 companies you chose as your "lab" companies: what they sell, to whom, how they make money

## Phase 2 — Financial Statement Analysis (Months 3–5)

**Goal:** Read an annual report cover to cover and know what matters, what's hidden, and what's suspicious.

### 2.1 Reading the annual report
Work through it in this order:
1. Chairman/CEO letter — note promises; check them next year
2. Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A)
3. Financial statements (standalone vs consolidated)
4. **Notes to accounts** — where the real information is
5. Auditor's report — look for qualifications, emphasis of matter, key audit matters
6. Related-party transactions, contingent liabilities, segment reporting
7. Corporate governance report, shareholding pattern, promoter/insider pledges

### 2.2 Ratio analysis
| Category | Key ratios |
|---|---|
| **Growth** | Revenue, EBITDA, EPS, FCF growth (YoY, CAGR) |
| **Profitability** | Gross margin, EBITDA margin, EBIT margin, net margin |
| **Returns** | ROE, ROCE, ROIC, ROA; DuPont decomposition |
| **Efficiency** | Asset turnover, inventory days, receivable days, payable days, cash conversion cycle |
| **Leverage** | Debt/Equity, Net Debt/EBITDA, interest coverage |
| **Liquidity** | Current ratio, quick ratio |
| **Cash flow** | CFO/EBITDA, FCF conversion, capex/sales, capex/depreciation |
| **Valuation** | P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B, EV/Sales, FCF yield, dividend yield, PEG |

### 2.3 Quality of earnings & forensic accounting
- Gap between reported profit and operating cash flow
- Aggressive revenue recognition, channel stuffing
- Capitalisation of expenses, changes in depreciation policy
- Rising receivables/inventory faster than sales
- Frequent "one-time" or "exceptional" items
- Off-balance-sheet liabilities, related-party transactions, auditor changes
- Beneish M-Score, Altman Z-Score, Piotroski F-Score

### 2.4 Sector-specific statements
- **Banks / NBFCs:** NIM, CASA, GNPA/NNPA, credit cost, CAR, provision coverage
- **Insurance:** Combined ratio, VNB, embedded value, persistency
- **Real estate / REITs:** NAV, FFO/AFFO, occupancy
- **Tech / SaaS:** ARR, NRR, CAC, LTV, Rule of 40

### Phase 2 resources
| Type | Resource |
|---|---|
| Book | *Financial Statement Analysis* — Martin Fridson & Fernando Alvarez |
| Book | *Financial Shenanigans* — Howard Schilit |
| Book | *The Interpretation of Financial Statements* — Benjamin Graham |
| Book | *Quality of Earnings* — Thornton O'glove |
| Practice | Annual reports of your 3 lab companies (last 5 years each) |

### Phase 2 deliverables
- [ ] 10-year ratio spreadsheet for each lab company
- [ ] Red-flag checklist (your own, refined over time)
- [ ] 2-page forensic review of one company's annual report

## Phase 3 — Financial Modeling & Valuation (Months 5–8)

**Goal:** Build a full forecast model and defend a target price.

### 3.1 Three-statement financial model
1. Historical data entry (5 years) — clean, standardised format
2. Revenue build — drive from fundamentals (volume × price, users × ARPU, stores × sales/store, segments)
3. Cost build — gross margin, operating expenses as % of sales or by driver
4. Working capital schedule (days-based)
5. Fixed asset & depreciation schedule
6. Debt & interest schedule (handle circularity)
7. Equity & dividend schedule
8. Balance sheet balancing check & cash flow integrity checks
9. Scenarios: bull / base / bear

### 3.2 Valuation methods
| Method | When to use | Key skills |
|---|---|---|
| **DCF (FCFF/FCFE)** | Stable, predictable cash flows | WACC, terminal value (Gordon growth vs exit multiple), sensitivity tables |
| **Trading comparables** | Most companies, sanity check | Peer selection, calendarisation, adjusting for growth/returns |
| **Precedent transactions** | M&A context | Control premiums, deal multiples |
| **Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP)** | Conglomerates, holding companies | Segment valuation, holdco discount |
| **Dividend discount model** | Banks, utilities, mature dividend payers | Payout and growth assumptions |
| **Residual income / P/B-ROE** | Banks & financials | Sustainable ROE vs cost of equity |
| **Reverse DCF** | Understanding what the market is pricing in | Implied growth/margins |
| **Asset-based / NAV** | Real estate, holding cos, resources | Asset-level valuation |

### 3.3 Key judgment areas
- Choosing the right risk-free rate, equity risk premium, and beta
- Terminal growth relative to nominal GDP growth
- Normalising earnings across the cycle (cyclicals, commodities)
- Probability-weighting scenarios
- Building a **variant view**: where and why do you differ from consensus?

### Phase 3 resources
| Type | Resource |
|---|---|
| Book | *Valuation: Measuring and Managing the Value of Companies* — McKinsey (Koller et al.) |
| Book | *Investment Valuation* and *The Little Book of Valuation* — Aswath Damodaran |
| Book | *Financial Modeling* — Simon Benninga |
| Free | Aswath Damodaran's NYU valuation course & datasets (pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar) |
| Paid | Wall Street Prep, Breaking Into Wall Street (BIWS), CFI (FMVA), or Training The Street |

### Phase 3 deliverables
- [ ] Full 3-statement model with 5-year forecast for each lab company
- [ ] DCF + comps valuation with sensitivity tables (WACC × terminal growth)
- [ ] Reverse DCF for one richly valued stock

## Phase 4 — Industry & Business Analysis (Months 8–10)

**Goal:** Understand *why* a company makes money and whether it can continue to — the qualitative edge that numbers alone don't give.

### 4.1 Strategic frameworks
- Porter's Five Forces
- Economic moats: network effects, switching costs, cost advantages, intangible assets, efficient scale
- Value chain analysis, industry life cycle
- Unit economics and operating leverage
- Capital allocation track record (M&A, buybacks, dividends, capex returns)

### 4.2 Management & governance assessment
- Track record of guidance vs delivery
- Insider ownership, pledging, related-party dealings, compensation structure
- Tone and consistency on earnings calls (read 8–12 quarters of transcripts)
- Board independence, auditor quality, minority shareholder treatment

### 4.3 Primary research ("scuttlebutt")
- Channel checks: distributors, dealers, retailers
- Customer and supplier conversations
- Expert networks, industry conferences, trade publications
- Job postings, app downloads, web traffic, pricing scrapes (alternative data)
- **Compliance note:** Never seek or use material non-public information (MNPI).

### 4.4 Sector knowledge — pick one to go deep
| Sector | What to master |
|---|---|
| Banking & financials | Asset quality cycles, rate sensitivity, regulation |
| IT services / software | Deal wins, utilisation, attrition, currency, SaaS metrics |
| Consumer / FMCG / retail | Volume vs price growth, distribution reach, brand strength |
| Pharma / healthcare | Pipeline, regulatory approvals, pricing, plant inspections |
| Industrials / capital goods | Order book, execution, commodity costs, government capex |
| Autos | Volumes, mix, EV transition, commodity pass-through |
| Energy / metals / commodities | Spreads, cost curves, cycle positioning |
| Telecom / media | ARPU, subscriber trends, spectrum, content costs |

| Type | Resource |
|---|---|
| Book | *Competitive Strategy* — Michael Porter |

## Phase 5 — Writing, Communication & Building a Portfolio (Months 10–12)

**Goal:** Produce professional-quality research that proves you can do the job.

### 5.1 Structure of an initiation report
1. **Page 1 summary:** Rating, target price, upside, 3-bullet thesis, key financials table
2. **Investment thesis:** 3–4 pillars, each supported by evidence
3. **Variant perception:** What does the market miss?
4. **Business overview** & industry context
5. **Financial analysis** & forecast discussion
6. **Valuation:** Primary + cross-check methods, sensitivity
7. **Catalysts:** With timeline
8. **Risks:** Specific, quantified where possible, and what would make you wrong
9. **Appendix:** Financial statements, model summary

### 5.2 Other formats to practise
- **Earnings preview / review note** (1–3 pages, turned around within hours)
- **Elevator pitch** (60 seconds) and **stock pitch** (5–10 minutes)
- **Thematic report** (e.g., a structural trend across a sector)
- **Short thesis** (requires extra rigor: what's broken and why the market doesn't see it)

### 5.3 Writing principles
- Lead with the conclusion (pyramid principle)
- One idea per paragraph; data supports every claim
- Charts > tables > text for showing trends
- Cut jargon; write for a busy PM who reads only page 1
- Always state what would change your view

### Phase 5 resources
| Type | Resource |
|---|---|
| Book | *The Pyramid Principle* — Barbara Minto |
| Book | *Best Practices for Equity Research Analysts* — James Valentine (**essential**) |
| Book | *On Writing Well* — William Zinsser |
| Practice | Read sell-side reports (available via broker accounts / university databases) and dissect their structure |

### Phase 5 deliverables
- [ ] 2 full initiation reports (15–30 pages each)
- [ ] 4 earnings notes (do them live, during actual results season)
- [ ] 1 recorded 10-minute stock pitch
- [ ] Public portfolio (blog, Substack, LinkedIn, or GitHub repository)

## 10. Certifications & Licensing

### Globally recognised
| Certification | Value | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst)** | ★★★★★ | 3 levels, ~300 hrs each, typically 2.5–4 years | Gold standard for research; Level I alone signals commitment |
| **CA / CPA / ACCA** | ★★★★ | 3–5 years | Huge advantage in accounting depth; very common among analysts |
| **FRM** | ★★★ | 2 parts | More risk-oriented; useful for financials coverage |
| **CAIA** | ★★ | 2 levels | Alternatives focus |
| **FMVA (CFI) / BIWS / WSP certificates** | ★★ | Weeks–months | Practical modeling proof; not a substitute for CFA |
| **MBA (Finance)** | ★★★★ | 1–2 years | Valuable for career switchers and networking |

### Regulatory licensing (depends on your market)
| Region | Requirement for publishing research |
|---|---|
| **United States** | FINRA SIE + Series 86 & 87 (Research Analyst); often Series 7/63 as well |
| **India** | NISM Series XV (Research Analyst) certification; SEBI RA registration if publishing independently |
| **United Kingdom** | CISI Investment Advice Diploma / IMC (Investment Management Certificate) commonly expected |
| **Other markets** | Check your local securities regulator's requirements for research analysts |

> **Recommended sequence:** Start CFA Level I during Phase 2–3; take your local research-analyst licensing exam in Phase 5; continue CFA Level II–III while working.

## 14. Building Your Research Portfolio

A public portfolio is the single strongest differentiator for candidates without a finance background.

### What to include
1. **2–3 initiation reports** — different sectors or one deep sector
2. **4–6 earnings notes** — shows speed and consistency
3. **1 sector primer**
4. **Financial models** (cleanly formatted Excel files)
5. **A track record** — dated calls with outcomes, including the wrong ones

### Where to publish
- Personal blog / Substack / Medium
- LinkedIn articles
- GitHub (models + any Python tooling)
- Investment communities: Seeking Alpha, ValuePickr, Value Investors Club (selective), Reddit communities
- Stock-pitch competitions: CFA Institute Research Challenge (students), university and fund pitch competitions

> **Disclaimer note:** If you publish recommendations publicly, include a clear disclaimer and check your local regulations — some markets require registration to publish stock recommendations.

## 15. Breaking Into the Industry

### Entry routes
| Route | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Research Associate (sell-side)** | Most common entry; work under a senior analyst |
| **KPO / research outsourcing firms** | Strong entry path for fresh graduates; builds modeling skills fast |
| **Internships** | Students; often convert to full-time |
| **Equity research at boutique brokers / PMS / family offices** | Smaller firms, broader responsibility |
| **Lateral moves** | From audit, credit analysis, consulting, corporate FP&A, or industry roles (sector experts are highly valued) |

### Interview preparation
**Technical questions to master**
- Walk me through the three financial statements / how they link
- How does a $10 increase in depreciation flow through the statements?
- Walk me through a DCF. How do you calculate WACC? Terminal value?
- When would you use EV/EBITDA vs P/E? Why can't you use EV/EBITDA for banks?
- What are the drivers of ROE (DuPont)?
- How do you value a loss-making company?

**Case/test formats**
- Modeling test (2–4 hours, build or update a model)
- Written test: write an earnings note from a press release
- **Stock pitch:** Have 2 long ideas and 1 short idea ready, each explainable in 2 minutes and defendable for 20

**Behavioural**
- Why research? Why this sector? Why this firm?
- Tell me about a stock you were wrong on and what you learned

### Networking
- Connect with analysts covering your sector on LinkedIn; reference their reports intelligently
- Attend CFA society events, investor conferences, and company AGMs
- Join investment clubs and online communities
- Ask for 15-minute informational conversations — come with specific, well-researched questions

**Modeling & valuation**

- [ ] Explain what valuation multiple suits which sector and why

**Business judgment**
- [ ] Articulate a company's moat (or lack of one) in 3 sentences

- [ ] State a variant view versus consensus with evidence

**Communication**
- [ ] Write an earnings note within a few hours of results
- [ ] Deliver a crisp 2-minute pitch and handle 20 minutes of questions
- [ ] Have a public portfolio of research to show

**Credentials**
- [ ] CFA Level I passed (or in progress)
- [ ] Local research-analyst licensing exam passed (if required in your market)

## 18. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Reading without building.** Ten books without a single model won't get you hired.
2. **Over-precision in models.** A 40-tab model with false precision is worse than a clean one with well-reasoned drivers.
3. **Anchoring on management's story.** Verify with numbers, peers, and primary research.
4. **Ignoring the balance sheet and cash flow.** Earnings can be managed; cash is harder to fake.
5. **No variant view.** Repeating consensus adds no value — know *why* you differ.
6. **Ignoring risks or downside scenarios.** Always know what would prove you wrong.
7. **Trying to cover every sector.** Depth in one sector beats shallow knowledge of ten.
8. **Not tracking your calls.** Without a feedback loop, judgment doesn't improve.
9. **Neglecting writing.** Brilliant analysis poorly communicated has zero impact.
10. **Compliance blind spots.** Never trade on or seek material non-public information; respect disclosure rules.
